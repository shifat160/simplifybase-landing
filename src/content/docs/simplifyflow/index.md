SimplifyFlow is in design. This page exists so the documentation URL is stable
from day one — bookmark it and it will fill in rather than move.

## What it will cover

- **Installation** — one plugin, what it adds to the admin, and what it stores
  in your database.
- **Channels** — public, private and read-only channels, and how WordPress
  roles and capabilities decide who can see and post in each.
- **Threads and mentions** — keeping a channel readable, and making sure the
  person who needs to see something actually does.
- **Direct messages** — one-to-one and small groups, and what an administrator
  can and cannot see.
- **Guests** — scoping an external account to a single channel, and expiring it
  when the work ends.
- **Notifications** — per-channel muting, digests, and how mail and browser
  notifications get delivered without flooding either.
- **Search and retention** — how far back search goes, and what you can prune.
- **Posting from elsewhere** — sending orders, posts, tickets and bookings into
  a channel, and posting a channel out to Slack or a webhook.

## Shaping it

The hard part is real-time delivery that feels instant on ordinary shared
hosting rather than only on a server you tuned yourself. That is the problem
this is being built around, and it is why there is no date on it yet.

If you run a team or a community from a WordPress site and have a view on where
a chat tool should stop, we want it.
