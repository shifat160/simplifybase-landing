SimplifyFlow is in design. This page exists so the documentation URL is stable
from day one — bookmark it and it will fill in rather than move.

## What it will cover

- **Installation** — one plugin, what it adds to the admin, what it stores in
  your database, and what it does to page load (nothing: chat traffic is kept
  off the rendering path).
- **Channels** — public, private and read-only channels, topics and pinned
  items, archiving, and how WordPress capabilities decide who can see, post in
  and manage each one.
- **Threads and mentions** — keeping a channel readable, mentioning a person, a
  role or a channel, and how notifications are derived from both.
- **Direct messages** — one-to-one and small groups, and what an administrator
  can and cannot see.
- **Calls** — huddles, video meetings, screen sharing, links for people with no
  account, and where recordings are stored.
- **Files and search** — uploads in the media library, search across messages,
  threads, files and site content, and the filters that make it usable.
- **Guests and spaces** — scoping an external account to one channel, expiring
  it, and running more than one workspace on a single install.
- **Events into channels** — sending orders, forms, posts, bookings and stock
  warnings into the channel that should see them.
- **Automations, webhooks and the API** — incoming and outgoing payloads,
  Slack-compatible formats, and the MCP server for AI agents.
- **Retention and export** — what is kept, for how long, and how to get all of
  it out.

## Shaping it

The hard part is real-time delivery that feels instant on ordinary shared
hosting rather than only on a server you tuned yourself. That is the problem
this is being built around, and it is why there is no date on it yet.

If you run a team or a community from a WordPress site and have a view on what
a chat tool must have on day one — and what it can do without — we want it.
